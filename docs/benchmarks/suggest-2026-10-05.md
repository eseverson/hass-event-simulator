# Suggest: real-data benchmark, 2026-10-05

Harness: `scripts/benchmark_suggest.py` (issue #26), run against a read-only copy of the user's recorder on Home Assistant 2026.9.4. Each automation that fired at least twice in the window is the ground truth: its first N fires (N = 1, 3, all, at most 500) are should-fire labels anchored at their start, and its referenced entities plus 3 random distractor entities are the tracks. A suggestion **reproduces** the automation when it hits at least 90 % of the real fires (2 min before to 10 min after) and at least 90 % of its own fires are real ones (fires while the real automation was off or did not exist yet are not counted). Held-out recall is the top suggestion's recall on the fires that were not labeled. Windows end 2026-10-06 03:00 UTC.

## What was wrong, and the fixes

- **Device triggers had no entity** (`50b1bcb`, `fix(history)`): Home Assistant's `referenced_entities` leaves out a device trigger whose entity is a registry id, so the entity that fires the automation never reached the panel. 9 of the user's automations use device triggers.
- **A few labels over-fitted conditions** (`1a6753d`): every unlabeled fire counted against a suggestion before simplicity, so with 1–3 labels the miner added whatever excluded the rest of the window ("only on Tue–Thu", "only between 22:20 and 22:25", "only when sensor.docker_printr_blue_status is Exited (0) 9 days ago"). Unlabeled fires now count only within the labeled stretch; fires past it break ties and are still listed as hints. Synthetic tests: `test_a_few_labels_generalize_instead_of_fitting_conditions_to_them`, `test_one_label_suggests_the_plain_trigger`. The weekday test now labels two weeks, so the weekend sits inside the stretch.
- **Values seen once, and toggles** (`9462a3b`): suggestions keyed on a value that never comes back (`scene.lights_off` changes to a timestamp, `person.evan`'s latitude, an `input_datetime`), and toggle automations (from/to on or off) were never suggested. A free-form entity now offers only recurring values plus a trigger on any change (`to: null`); anchors that follow changes in more than one direction get one trigger listing them (`to: [off, on]`).
- **`for:` at exactly `max_lag`** (`f5763da`): "off after an hour on" fires a moment after the hour, just past `max_lag` (1 h), so it came back as the switch turning off. Lags up to `max_lag` plus the tolerance now count.

## OR of triggers and any-change triggers (#33, #34)

Rerun on a fresh read-only copy, window end 2026-10-06 04:00 UTC (an hour later than the runs above, so the "before" numbers differ slightly from the first table). Before is `d769c97` (main, the state the sections above describe), after is `4c00939` plus the harness fixes:

- **OR of triggers** (`c36f41f`, #33): when no single trigger hits every anchor, ORs of up to three triggers (any entities and kinds) are built by greedy set cover over the anchors, from each of the best three triggers; each step adds the trigger, searched again around the anchors still missed, that hits the most of them with the fewest bad fires. A trigger joins only as evidence: it fires near the missed anchors more often than its fires would at random (binomial tail at 1 %), and one fitted to a single moment (a clock time, a threshold, a `for:`) never joins for one anchor or one burst of labels. The parts keep their side's trigger id, so a start/end pair can have an OR on one side. Each extra trigger costs as much simplicity as a condition. Synthetic tests: door or motion, a chatty sensor meeting a stray label, a clock time meeting a burst of labels, a start/end pair with an OR on the start side.
- **Any change of a numeric sensor or attribute** (`f7e8c46`, #34): a state trigger with no from/to (the blueprint controllers' shape; `to: null` too when some reports change attributes only), and the same on a numeric attribute whose state stays put. Every chatty sensor has a report within the tolerance of any anchor, so it is offered only when the anchors land on its reports (within 5 s) more often than chance; hand-drawn labels do not. The greedy conditions then add `numeric_state` above/below at fire time: "every report while above 72" comes back as the state trigger plus `numeric_state above: 72`, the template-free form of the fan controller's turn-on branch (synthetic test). `to: null` now reads "changes state" in explanations, a bare state trigger "changes".
- **Labeled periods and hint anchors by binary search** (`4c00939`): 500 labels and candidates firing 30 000 times made the linear label lookups 50 s of a 54 s mining run; now 4 s.
- **Harness**: Home Assistant 2026.9 trials an `http:` config moved out of YAML and restarts (dropping every websocket) unless it is confirmed within 5 minutes, which a sweep now outlasts (`7de6bee`); a kept copy keeps its window end, since the disposable instance records into it (`a1b233c`).

## Event triggers (#35)

Rerun on a fresh read-only copy, window end 2026-10-06 05:00 UTC; before is `5f700c5` (main), after is `c8cc907`. The recorder keeps every bus event (no `recorder: exclude: event_types` here): over the 30 days, 1 890 `zha_event`, 137 `android.zone_entered` / `zone_exited`, 40 `mobile_app_notification_cleared`, and Home Assistant's own bookkeeping (43 213 `automation_triggered`, 22 992 `service_registered`, 3 498 `call_service`, `*_registry_updated`, `recorder_*`, ...), which is left out.

- **Event triggers** (`2cc33bb`): the recorder's events of the window (Home Assistant's own left out, at most 20 000, the busiest types dropped first) go on the timeline beside the states. For each type with events near the anchors more often than chance, the miner offers the type alone and the `event_data` that singles out the anchored events (greedy over their scalar `(key, value)` pairs; of data picking out the same events, the fewest and best-known keys: a device id before a cluster id). Exact validation fires the types the suggestions listen to in the sandbox (`e5627b2`, scenario `bus_events`), and a replay of the user's own event-triggered automation now fires too (the panel sends the events of its types and shows them on an **Events** row, `c8cc907`): `temp_bidet_capture_rig` over Sep 11–12 replays its 14 real fires. Synthetic tests: two remotes sending random commands (device + command recovered, both commands of one remote leave the command out), a device whose id, cluster and command all pick out the same events (the id stays), a static string Home Assistant would parse as a number is never put in `event_data`.
- **Calendar**: `trash_reminder` fires 7 h before each `calendar.trash` event. The calendar entity turns on at the event's start, and no state trigger can fire before a state change (`for:` is never negative), so only a real calendar trigger reproduces it, and the sandbox has no calendar entity to serve one: deferred to #37. The clock stand-in ("17:00, only on Wed") stays exact on this window with 3 or all labels. A calendar on/off state trigger now reads "an event of calendar.x starts / ends".
- **Template**: `Turn off boot dryer` (a template on an `input_datetime` plus 24 h) did not fire twice in the window. Its template-free form is a time trigger at the entity with an offset: #38.

## Validation cost (#36)

The 108 s waits were the harness, not the integration: it scored each run on the event loop of the instance under test, and with 30 000 fires per suggestion its check that a fire fell while the real automation was enabled (one interval per recorded run of it) took 108 s, holding up every replay in flight. It now scores by binary search, in a thread. Validation now replays the best suggestions while their runs fit 40 000 (the rest keep the miner's estimate, `validation_capped`) and leaves out the placeholder action. Fan controllers, 30 d, `--jobs 2`, before (`5f700c5`, fixed harness) → after: living room N=500 20.3 → 7.0 s (validation 3.4 s; 1 of 8 suggestions replayed), every other fan run 3.6–6 s → 4–7.5 s; all still reproduce (top 1).

## Time at an entity, and the template note (#38)

- **Time trigger at an entity** (`87e6b54`): anchors a constant offset from the time an input_datetime (date and time, date only, or a time of day) or a `device_class: timestamp` sensor holds around them get `trigger: time` with `at: {entity_id, offset}`, offered when they fall near its fires more often than chance. The evaluator re-arms on every write of the entity as Home Assistant does (a time replaced before it is due never fires; an input_datetime's time may be now, a sensor's must be later; offsets add local wall time, so noon + 24 h across a DST change is 25 real hours), and the fidelity test checks it against the sandbox on both 2026.2.3 and 2026.9.4. Both releases take `at: <entity>` and `at: {entity_id, offset}`; 2026.2.3 reads `{entity_id}` without `offset` as a clock time and fails to attach it, so an offset of 0 renders as the plain entity id. `device_class: uptime` sensors are 2026.9-only and left out.
- **Template note**: when the best suggestion still misses anchors or fires where it should not, and the labels follow one tracked numeric sensor rising above another with the same unit (80 % of the anchors, more often than chance) better than it does, the explanations end with "The labeled moments line up with sensor.a rising above sensor.b; that needs a template trigger". No template is generated.
- **Benchmark**: `Turn off boot dryer` (the template on `input_datetime.boot_dryer_start_time` + 24 h) has not fired in the window: the helper still holds 2026-04-03 16:34. It is covered by synthetic tests only (`test_recovers_a_time_trigger_at_a_helper_plus_an_offset`, a helper set every 2–3 days, recovered from 1 or 3 labels; `test_a_time_only_helper_with_an_offset`). A regression sweep on a fresh read-only copy (window end 2026-10-06 06:00 UTC, `a859473` → `5cd1fb9` (now `87e6b54`)): all 135 runs reproduce exactly as before (top 1 and top 3) with the same top suggestion. Entity-time suggestions show up only lower in two single-label lists (rank 3 and 5: a backup sensor's and `sensor.sun_next_setting`'s time plus an offset, chance fits of one label), and no explanation carries the template note.

## Calendar triggers (#37)

- **Replay** (`c256094`): the sandbox serves calendar triggers from stub calendar entities (never in the state machine, so the calendar's recorded states still replay as recorded). The events are the scenario's `calendars` (the panel reads them with `calendar_events`: the live calendar's own through `calendar.get_events`, plus the events its recorded states name that it no longer returns), else the events the calendar's recorded states name (`message`, `start_time`, `end_time`, `all_day`: the current or next event, known days ahead, so a negative offset finds it). An end trigger on a round time fires too (an event ending where a 15-minute fetch span starts counts as active, as Home Assistant's demo calendar answers). The panel shows a read-only lane per calendar (`67f372b`).
- **Suggest** (`2098ddf`): anchors a constant offset from a tracked calendar's event starts or ends get `trigger: calendar` with `event` and `offset`; a `state` condition on the calendar's `message` keeps some of its events when the calendar names them at the fire, else the explanation names the template condition on `trigger.calendar_event.summary`. The evaluator adds offsets in local wall time, as Home Assistant does (checked against the sandbox on 2026.2.3 and 2026.9.4, a 48 h offset across the fall-back night included). Among candidates firing at the same instants a calendar trigger wins over a clock time, the smallest offset first.
- **Benchmark**: a fresh read-only copy, window end 2026-10-06 08:00 UTC, before `6ee4d00` → after `67f372b`. The disposable instance has no calendar integration (the copy's config entries are disabled), so `calendar.get_events` is not there and `calendar.trash`'s events come from its recorded states alone: the five Thursday pickups of the window, named days ahead (the entity was unavailable Sep 18-21, but the states before and after name every pickup). `trash_reminder`, 30 d: N=1 n → **Y** ("7 h before an event of calendar.trash starts", 4/4 real fires, precision 1.0, validated in the sandbox), N=3 and all stay Y with the calendar trigger as the top suggestion instead of "17:00, only on Wed". Every other run reproduces exactly as before (top 1 and top 3, all 135) with the same top suggestion; median 2.1 s, max 7.6 s, unchanged.

## Remaining gaps

- An OR takes at most three triggers, and parts are added greedily (track_entry_to_lemons_zone has eight).
- Template relations other than one sensor rising above another (a difference over a threshold) are not detected (#38).
- With the automation's own targets among the tracks, the top suggestion is often the action's effect (the light it turns on), which fires a few ms after the real trigger. The benchmark gives the miner the automation's referenced entities, targets included; in Suggest mode the user picks the tracks.
- A single label stays ambiguous: 12 of 28 reproduce (30 d), and the rest show the real fires as outside-fire hints to label.
